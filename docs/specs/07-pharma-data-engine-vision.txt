5. Verified Dwaprice Technical Details

Public documentation advertises:

- Approximately 14,700+ pharmaceutical product/pack records.
- Arabic and English names.
- Active ingredients, strengths, dosage forms, and manufacturers.
- Barcodes and ATC classifications.
- Current and historical prices.
- Incremental changes through "/price-changes".
- A free, keyless sample of 50 fixed products.
- Full catalog access and exports subject to plan restrictions.

Important implementation details:

- Incremental change delivery is at least once, so events must be processed idempotently.
- Rate limits and quotas must be respected.
- Barcodes may correspond to multiple records; never assume uniqueness.
- Sample endpoints are for validation, not full production coverage.
- Published paid plans include Starter at EGP 1,500/month, Business at EGP 6,000/month, and Enterprise starting at EGP 20,000/month. Reverify pricing before any commercial decision.

Start with free sample endpoints only.

No paid plan, API subscription, paid service, or commercial commitment may be activated without explicit owner approval.

6. Unified Product Data Architecture

Design a normalized master data system covering:

Product identity

- Internal immutable product ID
- Product category and subtype
- Arabic and English names
- Trade names and aliases
- Brand and manufacturer
- Distributor and supplier references
- Country-specific registration identifiers
- GTIN/EAN/barcodes, including multiple codes per product
- Pack sizes and units of measure
- Product images where appropriately licensed

Pharmaceutical information

- Active ingredients and combinations
- Ingredient strength and units
- Dosage form and administration route
- ATC classification where applicable
- Prescription or OTC classification
- Registration and market status
- Country-specific regulatory information

Commercial information

- Country and currency
- Official or published retail price
- Source-reported price
- Supplier purchase price
- Discounts and bonuses
- Tax treatment where applicable
- Price effective date
- Price observation timestamp
- Full price history

Data governance

- Source name and source-specific identifier
- Raw source reference
- Collection and verification timestamps
- Confidence and validation status
- Conflict history
- Approval and audit history

Keep supplier-specific identifiers separate from global product identity.

Never assume two products are identical merely because they share a name, ingredient, or barcode.

7. Multi-Country Architecture

Separate:

1. Global product concepts.
2. Country-specific registered products.
3. Country-specific pack presentations and identifiers.
4. Local regulatory and commercial information.
5. Country-specific prices, taxes, currencies, and sales rules.

A medicine available in Egypt and Saudi Arabia may have different registration numbers, pack sizes, prices, manufacturers, and regulatory statuses.

Never merge country-specific records without sufficient evidence.

Use a modular country-adapter design.

Initial active market: Egypt.

Future markets: Saudi Arabia, other GCC countries, and additional Arab countries.

Do not implement unnecessary international integrations during the Egyptian MVP.

8. Continuous Product Discovery

Cosmetics, supplements, and pharmacy supplies are continuously changing.

Build an extensible discovery pipeline using:

- Authorized supplier Excel/CSV files.
- Supplier catalogs.
- Purchase invoices.
- Barcode scans.
- Pharmacy-submitted product suggestions.
- Publicly accessible and permitted product information.

A product must not require complete pharmaceutical fields when those fields are not applicable.

9. Data Matching and Deduplication

Implement layered matching:

1. Country and regulatory identifiers.
2. Verified product and pack identifiers.
3. Barcodes, with collision detection.
4. Manufacturer, trade name, strength, dosage form, and pack size.
5. Arabic-English normalization.
6. Conservative fuzzy matching.

AI-assisted matching may suggest candidates but must not silently approve ambiguous records.

Never merge different strengths, formulations, ingredient combinations, or pack presentations without validated evidence.

Examples requiring explicit negative tests:

- Vitamin B6 versus B12.
- Omega-3 versus Omega-6.
- Different antibiotic strengths.
- Tablets versus suspensions.
- Different pack sizes.
- Similar Arabic trade names.

Record the rationale and provenance of every accepted match.

10. Pricing and Update Engine

Build a source-aware pricing system supporting:

- Initial imports.
- Scheduled or manually triggered synchronization.
- Incremental updates where available.
- Product additions, renames, cancellations, and status changes.
- Price history.
- Conflict detection.
- Stale-price alerts.
- Reconciliation between sources.
- Manual approval where required.
- Safe rollback.

Separate official/public retail prices from supplier prices, discounts, and actual transaction prices.

Never automatically overwrite pharmacy-specific selling prices or historical invoices because an external source changed.

A missing price is not zero.

A source-reported price is not automatically an official current price.

Preserve the original source, effective date, observation date, and confidence status.
